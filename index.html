BUYING GUIDE

How to choose a self-cleaning litter box.

Five checks before you spend $200 or more on an automatic box.

Read the full guide

Quick answer: Match the box to your cat’s size and confidence, look for sensors that pause cleaning when a cat steps in, and price in the liners and odor gel it uses.

  1. Size to your cat. Check the listed weight range and entry height. Low entries suit kittens, seniors and short-legged cats.
  2. Open vs. enclosed. Open tops (SC02, SC03, SC09) feel roomier for big or nervous cats; enclosed boxes contain scatter and smell better.
  3. Safety sensors. Look for weight or anti-pinch sensors that stop the cycle when your cat comes near.
  4. Running costs. Liners, deodorizing gel and spare parts add up — they’re in the carousel above so you can compare.
  5. Not ready for a motor? A large stainless steel box like the LB02 is easy to clean and needs no power.
DIRECT ANSWERS

Frequently asked questions.

Do self-cleaning litter boxes work for every cat?

Most models list a weight range for cats. Check it on the product page, and give shy cats a week or two with their old box nearby.

What ongoing costs should I expect?

Mainly liners, deodorizing gel and the odd spare part. The refills are in the same carousel so you can price them in up front.

Open-top or enclosed litter box?

Open designs feel less confining for large or nervous cats; enclosed designs hide scatter and odor better.

How does a GPS dog fence work?

You draw a boundary in the app and the collar uses GPS to tell when your dog nears it — there are no buried wires.
